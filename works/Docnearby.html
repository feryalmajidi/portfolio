DocNearby

DocNearby – Healthcare Discovery & Appointment Booking App for Expats in Vienna (Project for the BFI Vienna diploma program)

Role

End-to-End UX/UI Design (Research, Wireframing, Prototyping)

Project overview

Finding the right doctor in a new city can be stressful—especially when dealing with language barriers and confusing insurance rules. That’s why I designed DocNearby, a mobile app built to help expats in Vienna easily find, compare, and book appointments with healthcare providers who match their language and insurance needs.

Problem

Navigating the healthcare system as an expat comes with real friction:

  • Language Barriers: It's tough to understand German medical terms or find doctors who speak English or your native language.
  • Insurance Confusion: Figuring out which doctors accept your specific public or private insurance is often a guessing game.
  • Outdated Booking: Relying solely on phone calls during limited office hours makes scheduling slow and frustrating.
  • Lack of Transparency: Hard-to-find reviews and unclear doctor details make it difficult to build trust upfront.

As a result, finding the right doctor within the healthcare system can be a time-consuming, fragmented, and uncertain experience for users.

Solution

DocNearby brings everything together into a clean, intuitive mobile experience. It combines map-based discovery with smart filters—allowing users to sort doctors by specialty, distance, language, and insurance coverage in just a few taps.

The Design Process

Empathize & Research

To understand what expats in Vienna truly struggle with, I looked into their daily pain points and built a user persona.

User Persona:

Parya represents an expat professional dealing with local bureaucracy and language hurdles. Her struggles—like finding English-speaking specialists or figuring out restricted clinic hours—directly shaped the core features of DocNearby.

Mental Flow:

Users follow a specific path: "What is nearby?" → "Who can treat my condition?" → "Can I book quickly?"

Key Decision Factors:

Doctor reputation, language compatibility, insurance coverage, and real-time availability.

Competitive analysis:

I checked existing tools in Austria to see what works and what's missing. While current apps have great databases, they often feel cluttered, lack interactive maps, and don't make insurance (ÖGK vs. private) clear upfront. DocNearby fixes this by focusing on a clean, map-first, and transparent experience.

Challenges:
They do not support seamless interactive map navigation, lack intuitive multi-language filtering for non-German speakers, and often present a cluttered user experience filled with advertisements. Additionally, they lack up-front transparency regarding specific public insurance (ÖGK) vs. private selection and do not provide optimized localized radius searches tailored tightly for Vienna's individual districts.

Strengths
They offer a highly comprehensive database of active medical professionals in Austria, supported by extensive repositories of authentic patient reviews to establish provider credibility. Furthermore, they utilize powerful, real-time automated appointment booking algorithms with instant calendar syncing and automated push notifications to effectively eliminate missed time slots.

Define

Based on the research, I set four simple design rules to guide the project:

  • Clarity First: Making everything easy to understand, even for non-native German speakers.
  • Speed & Efficiency: Cutting down the steps needed to book an appointment.
  • Trust & Transparency: Showing insurance details and spoken languages right away.
  • Accessibility: Keeping contrast and touch targets friendly for everyone (following WCAG 2.1 AA standards).

Ideate

During ideation, I mapped out the structural framework and navigational pathways to ensure the solution feels natural and intuitive.

Information Architecture:

I kept the navigation simple with a bottom bar: Home, Discovery (Map), Appointments, and Profile. While the app targets Vienna with German labels, English is fully supported to help non-German speakers navigate smoothly.

  • Start (Home): Quick access to general search and featured providers.
  • Entdecken (Map): An interactive map view where users can define their location and filter results by radius (e.g., up to 14 km) to find the nearest clinics.
  • Termine (Appointments): A unified view to manage upcoming, past, and cancelled appointments.
  • Profil (Account): Management of personal profiles, preferences, and settings.

Key User Flows:

  • Discovery & Search Flow: View providers on the interactive map → Select results from the bottom cards → View provider details.
  • Appointment Booking Flow: View availability → Select date and time → Instant confirmation.
  • Appointment Management Flow: Navigate to appointments → Filter by status → Reschedule or cancel.

Prototype

Wireframes & UI: I started with quick sketches before moving to digital wireframes in Figma. The design uses a trustworthy medical blue combined with a clean, soft layout to reduce visual clutter on information-heavy screens like search filters and doctor cards.

Low-Fidelity Wireframes:

I began with paper sketches and low-fi digital wireframes to establish layout priorities. Onboarding fields were minimized to reduce drop-offs, filter screens were given clear hit states for quick selection, and doctor cards were structured to show language and insurance tags instantly.

Interactive Prototype:

I built a high-fidelity interactive prototype to test user navigation streams. This phase introduced vital functional elements, such as a single-selection specialty dropdown, a dynamic booking calendar, a mandatory booking data verification check ("Confirm booking"), and system fallback screens to handle alternative paths gracefully.

Style Guide:

The primary blue was chosen to signal trust and medical reliability while meeting WCAG AA contrast requirements against white backgrounds. White and soft grays keep the interface minimal, reducing visual noise on information-dense screens like provider listings and filters. The logo combines a location pin and a medical cross to represent discovering and connecting with nearby healthcare providers with precision. The concentric ripples at the base of the pin represent localized proximity, emphasizing the app's focus on radius-based, accessible searches.

Test

Usability test:

I tested the interactive prototype with four expats living in Vienna, asking them to find a local general practitioner and book an appointment.

The Big Insight: While users loved how clean and fast the filtering felt, they hit a moment of confusion at the final summary screen—the progress bar finished, but it didn't give an explicit "booking confirmed" message, leaving them unsure if their appointment went through.

The Fix: I updated the flow by adding a persistent booking-status card directly on the Home screen, giving users instant peace of mind without needing to dig through the appointments tab.

Updated user flow:

I've updated the original user flow to show that the user can verify their booking status more easily directly from the Home screen. Originally, the user was only able to view booking updates inside the dedicated appointments tab, but with further testing it was apparent they wanted to see their upcoming appointment details immediately upon opening the app considering there was a clear need for instant validation and peace of mind on the main dashboard as well.

Outcomes

  • Identified and resolved the primary point of confusion in the booking flow: users couldn't tell if their appointment was confirmed after reaching the summary screen.
  • Added a persistent booking-status card to the Home screen, giving users immediate confirmation without needing to navigate to the Appointments tab.
  • Delivered a complete design system covering typography, color, components, and accessibility guidelines aligned with WCAG 2.1 AA.

Conclusion:

Through this design process, I learned that transparency, accessible location-based information, and clear filtering are the ultimate keys to a successful and trustworthy healthcare product.
